Integrating Attachment Theory and Disaster Scenarios: A Multi-Generational Simulation

Overview

Traditional psychology generally regards "insecure attachment" (anxiety and avoidance) as a psychological defect, but this has raised an evolutionary paradox as to why these traits have not been eliminated by natural selection over millions of years.

Problem Statement

The Social Defense Theory (SDT) assumes that these traits provided advantages for group survival in ancient disaster environments (such as the anxious type as a "warning radar" and the avoidant type as an "escape guide"), but the current academic community is extremely lacking in generative mathematical proofs of cross-generational macro-evolution.

Research Approach

Therefore, this study innovatively combines evolutionary computing (EC) with multi-agent modeling (ABM) to simulate peaceful and disaster environments to observe the evolutionary trajectory of the group across generations. The ultimate goal is to verify through computational means whether harsh environmental pressures will naturally select groups with psychological heterogeneity, thereby providing direct code-level evidence for the evolutionary roots of the insecure attachment pattern.

Methodology

  • Evolutionary Computing (EC): Used to model the evolution of psychological traits over multiple generations
  • Agent-Based Modeling (ABM): Simulates individual behaviors and group dynamics
  • Environment Simulation: Creates both peaceful and disaster scenarios to test trait selection

Expected Outcomes

This research aims to provide computational evidence that psychological diversity, particularly the traits associated with insecure attachment, represents an adaptive strategy at the group level when facing environmental uncertainties and threats.
